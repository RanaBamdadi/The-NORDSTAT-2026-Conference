Understanding the annually laminated (varved) sediments
  • The sedimentation is build up by mineral grain and organic material
  • In the lake Kassjön it builds up 0.77 mm on average, yearly
  • In total about 5 meters of sediments which gives approximately 6400 years of sedimentation
  • Light-colored layers represent mineral grains, while darker layers correspond to organic accumulation.
  • Within- and between-year variations reflect the balance between minerogenic and organic material.

Interpretation

  • A pronounced light spring peak may indicate heavy snowfall during winter.
  • A low dark spring peak may suggest a less snowy winter.
  • A flatter, darker region after the spring peak may indicate a warmer summer.

Independent Error Model (IEM) (Arnqvist & Sjöstedt de Luna (2019))

\[ y_i(t_{ij}) = g_i(t_{ij})+\epsilon_i(t_{ij}), \qquad j=1,\ldots,n_i,\quad i=1,\ldots,N \]

\(\boldsymbol{y}_i(t)\) → The observed function

\(\boldsymbol{\epsilon}_i(t)\) → \(N_{n_i}(0,\sigma^2 \mathbf{I}_{n_i})\)

\[ g_i(t) = \boldsymbol{\phi}(t)^T \boldsymbol{\eta}_i, \qquad \boldsymbol{\eta}_i = \boldsymbol{\mu}_{z_i} + \boldsymbol{\gamma}_i \sim N_p(\boldsymbol{\mu}_{z_i},\Gamma_{z_i}) \]

\(\boldsymbol{\phi}(t) = [\phi_1(t), ..., \phi_p(t)]^T\)

\(z_i \rightarrow\) The unknown cluster membership

\(\boldsymbol{\mu}_{z_i} \rightarrow\) Expected spline coefficients for a cluster

\(\Gamma_{z_i} \rightarrow\) Covariance matrix of Random effects of spline coefficients

\(\boldsymbol{\gamma}_i\rightarrow\) Subject-specific within-cluster variability

  • The model parameters and cluster memberships are estimated using the EM algorithm.
  • Cluster assignments are then determined based on posterior probabilities.

    • Extension to Correlated Errors

    From IEM to DEM

    Dependent Error Model (DEM)

    The measurement error process \(\epsilon_i(t)\) is modeled as a zero-mean Gaussian process with exponential covariance

    \[ \operatorname{cov}\big(\epsilon_i(t), \epsilon_i(t+h)\big) = \sigma^2 e^{-\theta |h|}. \]

    For equidistant time points scaled to \([0,1], \quad t_{ij}-t_{i,j-1}=\frac{1}{n_i-1},\quad j=2,\ldots,n_i,\)

    \[\boldsymbol{\epsilon}_i \sim N_{n_i}(0,\sigma^2 T_i(\rho)),\]

    where \[ T_i(\rho) = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & \rho & \rho^2 & \cdots & \rho^{n_i-1}\\ \rho & 1 & \rho & \cdots & \rho^{n_i-2}\\ \vdots & \vdots & \vdots & \ddots & \vdots\\ \rho^{n_i-1} & \rho^{n_i-2} & \rho^{n_i-3} & \cdots & 1 \end{pmatrix}, \] is an AR(1)-type correlation matrix where \(\rho = e^{\frac{-\theta}{(n_i - 1)}}.\)

    Understanding the annually laminated (varved) sediments

    • The full dataset contains roughly 6,400 annual profiles, each corresponding to one year.
    • These profiles were extracted from digital greyscale images of the sediment core, where each pixel has a value between 0 and 255.
    • For each year, a five-pixel-wide section of the image is sampled, and the mean grey-scale values are calculated to produce the final annual profile.

    Interpretation

    • In spring, as the snow melts and runoff increase, minerogenic material is transported from the surrounding landscape into the lake. This produces a bright-colored layer in the sediment, representing high grey-scale values.
    • During summer, organic material produced mainly by lake organisms sinks to the bottom, forming a darker layer with lower grey-scale values.
    • In winter, under the ice cover, fine organic material is deposited, creating a thin dark layer, which is generally followed by the next spring’s bright mineral layer associated with snowmelt.

    Finally, Varves with similar yearly profiles correspond to similar weather conditions and each varve can be represented as a function, and we aim to group them into similar climate clusters by developing model-based functional clustering methods.

    Dependent Error Model (DEM)

    We transform the correlated residuals into independent residuals using a Cholesky transformation.

    Assuming \(\rho\) is known, we apply a Cholesky factorization,

    \[T_i^{-1}(\rho) = C_i^T(\rho)\, C_i(\rho),\]

    \[ C_i(\rho) = \frac{1}{\sqrt{1 - \rho^2}} \begin{pmatrix} 1 & -\rho &0 &\cdots &0 \\ 0 & 1 & -\rho &\cdots &0 \\ \cdots& \cdots & \cdots & \cdots &\cdots \\ 0 & 0 &0 & \cdots & \sqrt{1 - \rho^2} \\ \end{pmatrix}. \]

    • \(\tilde{\mathbf{y}}_i = C_i(\rho)\mathbf{y}_i\)
    • \(\tilde{\boldsymbol{\phi}}_i = C_i(\rho)\boldsymbol{\phi}_i\)
    • \(\tilde{\boldsymbol{\epsilon}}_i = C_i(\rho)\boldsymbol{\epsilon}_i\) \(\Rightarrow\tilde{\boldsymbol{\epsilon}}_i \sim N_{n_i}(0,\sigma^2 \mathbf{I}_{n_i})\)
    • Residual dependence is removed after transformation
  • The model parameters and cluster memberships are estimated using the EM algorithm.
  • Cluster assignments are then determined based on posterior probabilities.

    • Kassjön sediment simulation design

    Input:
    \(K=4\); 50 curves per cluster; \(n_k=50\); \(N=200\); \(\rho \in \{0.3,0.5,0.8\}\); \(\sigma=10\); Monte Carlo replications \(M\).
    Cluster-specific parameters \((\boldsymbol{\mu}_k,\Gamma_k)\), \(k=1,\ldots,K\), are obtained from the Kassjön sediment data under IEM.

    Output:
    ARI for each method and scenario.


    For each scenario \(\rho\):
      For \(m=1,\ldots,M\):
        For \(k=1,\ldots,K\):
          For \(i=1,\ldots,n_k\):

            Generate spline coefficients:
             \(\boldsymbol{\eta}_{ik}=\boldsymbol{\mu}_k+\boldsymbol{\gamma}_{ik},\quad\boldsymbol{\gamma}_{ik}\sim N_p(\mathbf{0},\Gamma_k)\)

            Construct latent curve:
             \(g_{ik}(t)=\boldsymbol{\phi}(t)^\top\boldsymbol{\eta}_{ik}\)

            Generate correlated measurement errors:
             \(\boldsymbol{\epsilon}_{ik}\sim N_{n_i}(\mathbf{0},\sigma^2T_i(\rho))\)

            Observe functional data:
             \(y_{ik}(t)=g_{ik}(t)+\epsilon_{ik}(t)\)

      Fit IEM-full, IEM-diag, DEM-full, DEM-diag, funFEM, and fdakmeans.
      Compute ARI.

    Waveform simulation design

    Input:
    \(K=3\); 50 curves per cluster; \(n_k=50\); \(N=150\); \(t\in[1,21]\); \(\rho \in \{0.3,0.5,0.8\}\); \(\sigma=0.7\); Monte Carlo replications \(M\).

    Output:
    ARI for each method and scenario.

    Define waveform basis functions: \(h_1(t)=\max(6-|t-11|,0)\), \(h_2(t)=h_1(t-4)\), \(h_3(t)=h_1(t+4)\).


    For each scenario \(\rho\):
      For \(m=1,\ldots,M\):
       For each cluster \(k = 1,2,3\):
        For \(i=1,\ldots,n_k\):

          Generate mixing variable:
            \(u \sim U(0.3,0.7)\)

          Construct class-specific latent curves:
             If \(k =1: z_i =1\) and \(\mu_i(t)=u h_1(t)+(1-u)h_2(t)\) for cluster 1
             If \(k =2: z_i =2\) and \(\mu_i(t)=u h_1(t)+(1-u)h_3(t)\) for cluster 2
             If \(k =3: z_i =3\) and \(\mu_i(t)=u h_2(t)+(1-u)h_3(t)\) for cluster 3

          Generate correlated Gaussian noise:
            \(\boldsymbol{\epsilon}_i(t)\sim N(0,\sigma^2T_i(\rho))\)

          Observe functional data:
            \(\boldsymbol{y}_i(t)=\boldsymbol{\mu}_i(t)+\boldsymbol{\epsilon}_i(t)\)

          Standardize each curve.

      Fit IEM-full, IEM-diag, DEM-full, DEM-diag, funFEM, and fdakmeans.
      Compute ARI.

    Functional signal–noise simulation design

    Input:
    \(K=3\); 50 curves per cluster; \(n_k=50\); \(N=150\); \(\sigma \in \{3,5,7\}\); Monte Carlo replications \(M\).

    Output:
    ARI for each method and scenario.

    For each scenario \(\sigma\):
      Generate K sinusoidal basis signals:
      \(\boldsymbol{S}(t) = (S_1(t), S_2(t), \ldots, S_K(t))^\top\)

      For \(m = 1, ..., M\):

        For each cluster \(k = 1, ..., K\):

          Generate cluster-level random effect:
          \(\boldsymbol{\gamma}_k \sim N(0, \theta^2 I_K)\)

          For each curve \(i = 1, ..., n_k\):

           Generate curve-level random effect:
           \(\boldsymbol{\delta}_i\sim N(0, \tau^2 I_K)\)

           Construct latent functional curve:
           \(\boldsymbol{\mu}_i(t) = (\boldsymbol{\gamma}_k+ \boldsymbol{\delta}_i)^{T} \boldsymbol{S}(t)\)

           Generate smooth Gaussian noise:
           \(\boldsymbol{\epsilon}_i(t) \sim GP(0, C(t,t')),\) \(\operatorname{Cov}(\epsilon(t),\epsilon(t'))=\sigma^2\exp\!\left[-\frac{(t-t')^2}{2\ell^2}\right]\)

           Observe functional data:
           \(\boldsymbol{y}_i(t) = \boldsymbol{\mu}_i(t) + \boldsymbol{\epsilon}_i(t)\)

    Fit IEM-full, IEM-diag, DEM-full, DEM-diag, funFEM, and fdakmeans.
    Compute ARI.
